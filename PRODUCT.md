# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

The owner, a general surgery resident, is the only user. Phone and laptop are used equally: a playbook minutes before a case, a topic between cases or on call, longer study at a desk. Pages are also read aloud by the device, so the spoken page matters as much as the visual one.

## Product Purpose

A private reference for case preparation and exam study: SCORE-aligned topics and operative playbooks. Success is reaching the right page and its answer in a few seconds, then reading it without fighting the interface.

## Positioning

Orient first, then read. The high-yield point is on top and everything else is one tap away.

## Brand Personality

Calm, exact, quiet. Plain clinical language, sentence case, the fewest words that work, nothing said twice. The product is named Pocket Chief, with no tagline and no "atlas". One noun per concept: Saved (bookmarked topics or playbooks), Studied (reader progress), Topics, Playbooks. "Reviewed" is for editorial approval only. "Library" is a developer word and never appears in the UI.

## Anti-references

- Identical icon-tile card grids.
- Thick side-stripe callouts.
- Glass blur on the shell.
- Small uppercase eyebrows above headings.
- Chrome that restates a default, such as a "Supported" label on every block.
- Developer copy in the UI (file paths, commits, implementation notes).
- A page header that takes half a phone screen.

The positive reference is the AMBOSS mobile reader: search on every page, a high-yield summary on top, an outline one tap away, a bottom toolbar, staircase bullets with underlined key terms, tables that read on one screen, obvious green and red callouts.

## Design Principles

1. Orient first: search on every page, the summary on top, the outline one tap away, page controls within thumb reach on phone.
2. Mark exceptions, not defaults. "Unsupported" shows as a symbol with a one-word accessible name; supported blocks show and say nothing.
3. Quiet to read and to listen to. Decorative icons are hidden from assistive tech, labels are never repeated, and the page adds no words beyond the content.
4. Phone and laptop are equal. Nothing important lives on only one.
5. One noun per concept, as listed under Brand Personality.
6. Figures belong in the reading view. This is a future capability, not part of the current review.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast in light and dark. The theme follows the device, with a manual override. Read-aloud is a supported mode.
